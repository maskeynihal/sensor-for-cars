HJ60 coolant temperature gauge: how the circuit works

Sources: Toyota FSM pages 13-73 and 13-74 (BJ, HJ series), and your measurements. The truck has a 12 V system with one battery. Click a part of the drawing, or use the guided tour.

1. The three parts

The sender (in the engine)

The sender is a brass plug in the cylinder head. Its tip is in the coolant. It contains a thermistor. A thermistor is a resistor that changes its resistance with temperature.

When the coolant gets hotter, the resistance decreases. FSM values: 226 Ω at 50 °C, 26 Ω at 115 °C.

The sender has one wire (yellow-green, "YG"). The engine block is the other connection (ground). The sender does not make a voltage. It only controls how much current can flow.

The name "sender" comes from its function: it sends the temperature to the dash, as a resistance.

The gauge (on the dash)

The gauge is the needle on the dash. Behind the needle is a bimetal strip. A bimetal strip has two metals that are bonded together. It bends when it gets hot. A small heater wire (25 Ω, terminals E and F) is around the strip.

More current in the heater makes the strip hotter. The strip bends more, and the needle moves toward H.

The gauge is slow by design. It needs some seconds to get hot or cool. Thus it shows the average current.

The voltage regulator (hidden inside the fuel gauge)

The regulator is a small bimetal switch. It opens and closes again and again, like the flasher unit of a turn signal.

The battery voltage is 12.9 V with the engine off and 14 V with the engine running. Without the regulator, the needle would move up each time you start the engine.

The regulator keeps the average voltage at about 7 V at all battery voltages. The gauge shows the average of the pulses, so the needle stays steady.

This is the cause of the changing numbers on your multimeter. The meter measured a voltage that switches on and off.

5. How the ESP32 reads it (option A)

Why two wires, not one

The gauge heater (25 Ω) and the sender are in series. The feed voltage divides between them. The part of the voltage across the sender depends only on the two resistances:

k = VF ÷ VE = Rs ÷ (Rs + 25)

The ESP32 measures E and F at the same time. It ignores the moments when the regulator is off. It calculates Rs = 25 × k ÷ (1 − k), and then it gets °C from the sender curve. The battery voltage and the pulses have no effect on the result.

Why it doesn't upset the dash gauge

Each tap goes through a 68 kΩ + 10 kΩ divider. This resistance is thousands of times more than the gauge circuit. The tap uses much less than 1% of the current, so the needle does not move.

The divider also decreases the voltage by 7.8 times. Thus 14 V becomes 1.8 V. This is safe for the ESP32 (maximum 3.3 V).

Do not use pin A. Pin A is before the regulator. It stays on while the sender wire goes to 0 V. Click "ESP32 tapped at pin A" above to see the result.

6. Other ways to connect (options A, B and C)

Select an option at the top of section 2. The drawing, the tour, the readouts, the scope and the multimeter change with the option. Click "ESP32 fails" to see what the driver gets.

Option B: how the ESP32 measures

The ESP32 switches 3.3 V through a Schottky diode and a 100 Ω reference resistor to the sender. It measures the voltage above the resistor (Vtop) and at the sender (Vs).

Rs = 100 × Vs ÷ (Vtop − Vs)

Near 100 °C, Vs is about 0.94 V and changes by about 19 mV for each °C. The dash gauge does not operate.

Option C: how the ESP32 operates the gauge

The gauge needs current, not a voltage. A MOSFET and a 20 Ω resistor connect F to ground. The ESP32 switches them on and off fast. The slow gauge uses the average heat.

D = ((25 + 20) ÷ (25 + Rs))²

At 100 °C, D is about 48%. A normally closed relay returns to the factory circuit if the ESP32 fails or finds a sender fault.

7. Words in this page

WordMeaning here
SenderThe temperature sensor in the engine. It is a resistor that changes with temperature.
Gauge (receiver)The temperature needle on the dash. The FSM uses the name "receiver gauge".
BimetalTwo metals that are bonded together. Heat makes one metal expand more than the other, so the strip bends.
NTC thermistorNegative temperature coefficient. The resistance decreases when the temperature increases.
E, FThe two terminals of the gauge heater. E is on the feed side. F is on the sender side.
FeedThe voltage at terminal E, from the regulator.
Pin AThe cluster connector pin that supplies the regulator. It is before the regulator. Do not connect a tap to it.
GroundThe metal of the truck (body and engine block). The current goes back to the battery through it.
DividerTwo resistors that make a voltage smaller by a constant ratio.
PWMPulse-width modulation. A switch turns on and off fast. The duty (on time ÷ total time) sets the average.
Relay (normally closed)A switch that a coil operates. With no coil power, the "normally closed" contacts connect.
Ratio kSender wire voltage ÷ feed voltage. It depends only on the temperature. The battery voltage and the pulses have no effect on it.
